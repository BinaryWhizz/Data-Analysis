Fake vs Real News Detection Dataset


DATA ANALYSIS REPORT

1. Data Cleaning Summary
- No missing values found
- No duplicate issues observed
- Data types are correct
- Dataset size: 1000 rows, 10 columns
- Label converted to numeric for analysis (fake=0, real=1) 


2. EDA (Key Findings)

A. Class Distribution
- Real news ≈ 48.5%
- Fake news ≈ 51.5%

B. Source Credibility vs Label
Correlation: +0.58

- Higher credibility → more real news
- Strong positive relationship 

C. Number of Citations vs Label
Correlation: +0.77 (strong)

- Articles with more citations are highly likely to be real
- This is one of the strongest indicators

D. Clickbait Score vs Label
Correlation: -0.90 (EXTREMELY STRONG)

- Higher clickbait → strongly associated with fake news
- This is the most powerful feature in dataset

E. Sentiment Score
Correlation: ~ 0 (no relationship)

- Sentiment does not influence fake vs real

F. Readability Score
Correlation: ~ 0 (very weak)

- Readability is not a strong indicator

G. Author Analysis
Author	               Real News Ratio
staff_writer	           ~50%
verified_journalist	       ~49%
unknown_source	           ~45% 



Final Conclusion

Fake news is strongly characterized by high clickbait, low credibility, and fewer citations, while real news tends to have higher credibility 
and more references. Traditional text features like sentiment or readability are not reliable indicators.
